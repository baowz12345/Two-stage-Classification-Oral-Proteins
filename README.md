The oral microecosystem is one of the most complex and diverse microbial communities in the human body. Microorganisms that colonize the mouth are closely linked to the development of various diseases, including oral inflammation, mucosal disorders, periodontal diseases, dental caries, and oral cancers. Beyond causing local oral health problems, oral microbiota can also affect endocrine balance, digestion, and neurological functions. However, researches confronted technical challenges, such as handling large amounts of complex biological data and addressing imbalanced datasets. These challenges limit the effectiveness of traditional experimental approaches and create obstacles for comprehensive studies of oral microorganisms and their links to disease. To deal with these challenges, we proposed a two-stage classification method to deal with the such classification issue in the field of oral microorganisms. Such method employed several features to classify the five oral microorganisms. The initial stage of this research adopted 18 distinct feature extraction approaches and incorporated 19 state-of-the-art classification algorithms. The combination of these two sets of research elements yielded 342 unique classification schemes for subsequent analytical experiments. Following completion of the first-stage screening, optimal feature subsets and high-performance classification algorithms with strong application potential were preliminarily filtered to support secondary-stage validation. In the second experimental stage, four superior algorithm frameworks and four optimized feature combinations were matched and applied to address identical classification tasks. Through comprehensive performance comparisons across all experimental groups, the approach with the best overall performance for each classification task was ultimately selected as the final analytical model.
